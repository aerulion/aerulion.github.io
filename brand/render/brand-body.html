01 / 08

The system

Six rules. They are the whole identity — if a change breaks one of them, it belongs somewhere else. Every asset in section 07 was built by following them and nothing more.

Constitution

  1. i

    Two colours

    Exactly #000 and #fff. No greys, no tints, no alpha on text. Hierarchy comes from size, position and density — never from dimming.

  2. ii

    30° and 60°, always

    Both angles are taken from the mark. Panel corners bevel at 30°; the cursor lattice is a 60° triangular grid. There is no third angle in the system.

  3. iii

    Nothing spins in the plane

    An in-plane rotation is off-grid for all but six frames of its cycle, so motion runs along the geometry instead. The one figure that turns does it in four dimensions and projects back down, so every silhouette it passes through is still built from the same four lattice directions.

  4. iv

    Hairlines are the layout

    A 1px rule does the work a box, a card or a background would do elsewhere. Fills are for the mark and for inverted buttons; nothing else gets a surface.

  5. v

    Instrumentation labels

    Small uppercase mono readouts — Node / 001, Status / Active, 02 / 03 — frame each block, the way a print registration mark or a telemetry panel would.

  6. vi

    Falloff is density, not opacity

    Where something fades, it thins: the lattice dithers vertices and segments in and out so the field never renders a grey pixel. This is law i restated for anything generative.

02 / 08

Colour

The palette is complete. There is no accent, no secondary, no tint ramp, and no semantic colour — a warning reads as a warning because of what it says and where it sits.

Palette

Ground#000000

The page, every panel, and the ground behind the mark. Never softened to a near-black.

Ink#FFFFFF

Type, hairlines, the mark, and the inverted button fill. Never softened to a near-white.

The one sanctioned exception is structural opacity inside a single generative figure — the unfold shells behind the mark carry an alpha so the stack reads as depth rather than as four overlapping outlines. It never applies to text, hairlines, or the mark's own silhouette.

03 / 08

Geometry

Two angles, four axes, one bevel. The lattice behind the masthead is the system running live: move the pointer over it and the grid deflects, thinning around the type instead of dimming.

Construction

Four axes

Every edge of the mark classifies onto one of these four directions. They are the only directions any line in the system may take.

The bevel

Top-left and bottom-right corners only, cut at 30°. The vertical drop is always the horizontal run times tan 30°.

The lattice

A 60° triangular grid on a 40px cell, row pitch 40 · sin 60°. It bends around real content rather than sitting behind it.

04 / 08

The mark

An equilateral triangle with a bolt cut through it. It has two states — drawn as an outline, and inked solid — and one motion, the unfold.

Usage

Inked / primary

The resting state. Use this everywhere a single static mark is needed.

Drawn / quiet

For large sizes and calm surfaces — the lockscreen, a watermark, the first beat of the draw-then-ink intro.

Clear space

One sixth of the mark's height on every side, and nothing but ground inside it — not a hairline, not a label.

The unfold

The outline is classified into the four lattice directions, lifted into 4D, rotated there, and projected back. That is why the mark can turn without ever spinning in the plane, and why every silhouette on the way through is still made of 30° and 60° edges. Two arcs of the same direction close a full turn, so a loop built that way has no seam.

06 / 08

Motion

Motion is construction, not decoration: things draw themselves, ink themselves, and deflect. Nothing eases in from nowhere and nothing bounces.

Behaviour

Draw, then ink

A figure arrives as a stroke tracing its own outline, then a solid sweep fills it from below. The stroke is clipped to wherever the ink is not, so there is no rim and no gap.

Deflect, don't dim

The lattice reads the live DOM and bends around it. Near an obstacle it drops vertices and segments; it never fades them.

Turn in 4D

The only rotation permitted. In-plane spin is banned outright — for a shape made of four fixed axes it is off-grid almost the entire time.

08 / 08

Do and don't

Most of the ways this system breaks are additions, not mistakes — a grey, a radius, a shadow, a rotation. Each one is individually harmless and collectively fatal.

Verdicts

Do

  • +Let hairlines run off the edge of the canvas.
  • +Thin a field out when it should recede.
  • +Label a block with a mono readout that says something true.
  • +Bevel a panel at 30°, top-left and bottom-right.
  • +Invert — black on white — to make something loud.
  • +Leave the mark inked and still when in doubt.

Don't

  • −Introduce a grey, a tint, or text at reduced opacity.
  • −Round a corner, add a shadow, or fill a card.
  • −Rotate the mark in the plane, at any angle.
  • −Draw a line at an angle that is not 30° or 60°.
  • −Set a heading in Tektur or a label in anything but mono.
  • −Put a frame on an asset whose crop you don't control.
